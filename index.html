Questions

Good to know.

Is Orbit free?

Yes. Orbit is free during the beta, with no ads and no account.

Which phones and Macs does it work with?

Any phone running Android 10 or later, and any Mac with Apple silicon running macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Both need to be on the same Wi-Fi.

Does my data go through a server?

No. Your phone and your Mac talk to each other directly on your network, encrypted end to end. There’s no Orbit cloud.

Can I talk through my Mac during a call?

Not yet — today you can see, answer, decline and dial calls from your Mac while the conversation stays on your phone. Call audio on your Mac is next on our list.

Can I call and text from my Mac?

Yes — since Orbit 0.10. Open the Orbit window on your Mac: Calls has your recent calls and a keypad, Contacts has everyone, and Messages brings texts and chats together. Calls and texts go out through your phone. Orbit asks on the phone once to allow contacts, call history and texts; it all stays between your phone and your Mac.

Can I use my phone from my Mac?

Choose Show phone in Orbit’s menu and your phone appears in a window on your Mac — tap, type and scroll with your mouse and keyboard. Android asks once each time you start (“Start now”).

Can I copy on my phone and paste on my Mac?

Yes — since Orbit 0.9.1, copying works both ways. Android only lets the app on screen read what you copy, so Orbit asks for one switch (“Orbit — copy to your Mac”) the first time. It only looks at what you copy, never your screen, and skips passwords.

How do updates work?

Orbit keeps itself up to date. On your Mac you’ll see the familiar “A new version is available” window; on your phone, a quiet card with an Update button. One tap and you’re on the latest — your connection and history stay put.

Why does macOS ask me to confirm the first launch?

The beta isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks once. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and choose “Open Anyway”. You won’t see it again.
